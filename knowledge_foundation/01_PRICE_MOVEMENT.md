# 01 — Price Movement

## 1.1 What the concept represents

Price movement, here, is the time-indexed sequence of prices and the segments, extensions and retracements a human or program can describe from it. It is the substrate for every later file. Most analytical errors in the later stages trace back to a choice made here about *what the unit of movement is* and *at what scale it is viewed*.

## 1.2 What is directly observable

**A-level (direct) observations** from a bar-based feed:

- For each bar: open, high, low, close, bar timestamp, and bar duration. Volume only if recorded [PM-01].
- The order of bars in time.

**Not directly observable from OHLC bars:**

- The order of trades and quotes within a bar.
- Which of the bar's high or low was printed first [PM-07].
- Resting orders, stop orders, or the identity of any participant.

## 1.3 Derived measurements (B) and representations (C)

Typical B measurements: bar range (high−low), body (|close−open|), direction (close vs open), upper and lower wick lengths, true range, and rolling volatility estimates. Each is deterministic once the bar is known.

Typical C representations: a sequence of alternating turning points (zigzag), segments between turning points, a run-length posterior (how long since the last change), a multi-resolution aggregation (e.g., 1-minute bars rolled into 5-minute, hourly and daily bars).

These are not market facts. They are organizing views. Two valid representations of the same bars can imply different "legs" (see §1.6).

## 1.4 How candidate movement elements are generated

Movement elements are generated by an operational definition (D). Example families, each scoped and each with unresolved parameters:

- **Directional run:** consecutive bars with close greater than prior close (or open-to-close direction). Parameter: how to treat inside bars and doji bars.
- **Displacement-like move (community term):** a run whose net range or body is large relative to a locally defined scale. Community sources use the word "displacement" without a single numeric definition. **Any numeric threshold is an unresolved parameter** (see CF-08). The primary ICT FVG and OB text read in this session uses "move" and "delivery"; the word "displacement" was not verified in the primary text read.
- **Leg:** the price path between two chosen turning points (see file 02). Leg endpoints depend on the swing definition, so a leg is a derived element, not a fact.
- **Movement scale:** the bar resolution on which the above are computed.

## 1.5 Extension, retracement, continuation

These are relations between legs, not states of the market.

- **Retracement** of leg B after leg A: the portion of A that B reverses. Requires that A and B are defined. Depends on swing selection (file 02).
- **Extension:** a leg that exceeds the prior leg's end. Whether this is "continuation" or "failure" depends on the structure view (file 03) and the scale.
- **Continuation:** a later leg in the same direction as the prior leg. Any statement that movement is "continuing" is an interpretation over a chosen representation, labeled F or E depending on who says it.

The defensible statement is: *"Under definition D with parameters P, leg 2 retraced 38% of leg 1 by close."* Adding "so the market is in a pullback" is an interpretation, not an observation.

Note: percentages of retracement (e.g., 50% "mean threshold") appear in ICT-related community sources and in the ICT Order Blocks primary lesson read here as a reference point for stop placement within a block (E). The primary text used "the 50 percent of the order block total range" for a stop-related location, not as a probabilistic claim (E). No evidence was verified that such retracement levels predict outcomes (CF-07).

## 1.6 Observation scale changes representation

The same price series at different scales can produce:

- Different turning points (a fine-scale pivot may be interior to a coarse-scale leg).
- Different wicks (aggregation takes the max high and min low of contained bars, so a coarser bar's wick can come from a single fine bar).
- Different sets of "obvious" levels (prior-day high exists only at daily resolution).

**Consequence:** the scale is part of the representation and must be stored with each derived element. Calling an element "the swing high" without naming its scale and definition is a non-reproducible statement.

Counterexample (symbolic, hypothetical): On a 1-hour chart, a turning point T1 looks like a clear reversal. On a 4-hour chart, T1 is inside a bar whose range covers the prior leg. Neither view is wrong; they answer different questions. A system that always uses the finest scale will generate many more candidate reversals than a system using coarse scale.

## 1.7 Intra-bar ordering (PM-07)

For any bar that contains both a high and a low, OHLC data does not say which came first. This affects questions such as:

- Did price take buy-side liquidity before or after it made a bullish displacement inside the same bar?
- Did a sweep and a reclaim occur within the bar?

Options a future system must choose among, each with a cost:

1. Mark such questions "undetermined at this scale" (safest; loses information).
2. Refer to a finer scale if available (requires that finer data be present and causal).
3. Use a rule (e.g., "assume direction of close") — this is an assumption (D/F) and must be stored as such.

No option is universally correct. The choice must be recorded and tested.

## 1.8 Change in movement behavior

Two different things are often conflated:

- **Change in the parameters of a process** (volatility, drift, trend) — a statistical change-point concept.
- **Change in a labeled market state** (e.g., "now in distribution") — an interpretation.

Statistical methods address the first. Verified methods:

- **Bayesian Online Change Point Detection (BOCPD)**: online, computes the posterior distribution over the run length (time since the last change) using a message-passing recursion, for models where parameters before and after a change are independent [OS-01]. It is causal: at time t it uses data up to t only.
- **PELT (Pruned Exact Linear Time)**: an offline method that finds the optimal segmentation under a penalty, with pruning giving linear cost under conditions [OS-02]. It uses the full series and is therefore **not causal** for live use; the `ruptures` documentation describes PELT as seeking the optimal segmentation under a pruning rule, with cost depending on conditions on the change-point layout [OS-03].

A statistical change point is not a market structure break and is not, by itself, a reversal. The relation between them must be a separate, tested claim (LL-03, CF-03).

## 1.9 Information availability

At time t, the available movement information is: all bars closed by t, plus the currently forming bar's partial state (if the system is live). Any "turning point" whose definition uses bars after t is **not available** at t. This is the basis for knowability tracking (file 00 §0.7; file 02).

## 1.10 Alternatives that remain legitimate

- Fine-scale and coarse-scale views of the same movement.
- Close-based versus wick-based movement measures.
- Offline segmentation (for research labeling) versus online change detection (for causal use).

None of these is universally "correct." The correct one depends on the task (file 11).

## 1.11 Insufficient information

- A single bar cannot establish a direction change, a leg, or an extension.
- A bar series without timestamps or with gaps cannot be assigned a scale reliably.
- Without volume or order-book data, no statement about participation, absorption or flow is supported. Price-only "absorption" claims are interpretations (E or F).

## 1.12 How new observations change interpretation

- A new bar can change the endpoint of a provisional leg (repaint risk if definitions depend on future bars). Every derived element must carry its knowability time so that revised interpretations are logged, not silently overwritten.
- A new bar that closes beyond a previous extreme can invalidate a prior "leg end" definition. The earlier output remains a historical fact about what was known then.

## 1.13 Common incorrect applications

- Calling a provisional pivot "confirmed" before its confirmation window has elapsed.
- Computing a retracement from a leg whose endpoint was chosen with hindsight.
- Treating an offline segmentation boundary as a live structural break.
- Mixing bars from different scales in one comparison without stating the scale.

## 1.14 Concrete counterexamples

- **Hypothetical:** A pivot is defined as the highest high of the last 5 bars on each side. At bar 10, bar 8 looks like a pivot high, but bars 9 and 10 are the only information after it. Bar 8 is not knowable as a confirmed pivot until bar 13 closes. A system using bar 8 as an entry reference at bar 10 uses future information.
- **Hypothetical:** A daily bar's high is above yesterday's high. Whether this "takes" a prior high depends on whether it occurred before or after the 1-hour sweep decision time, which the daily bar alone does not reveal (PM-07).

## 1.15 Source-grounded application

The primary ICT Order Blocks lesson (P1) describes a support or resistance reference on a higher timeframe ("monthly, weekly or daily") and waiting for price to trade into it before interpreting the down candle (E). This shows that the source treats scale as part of the reference, not as an incidental choice. Details in `08_APPLIED_CHART_CASES.md`.

## 1.16 Implications for a causal implementation

- Store OHLC with bar-close timestamps; never use the forming bar as a closed observation.
- Store the scale and definition with every movement element.
- Store `knowable_at` for each derived element.
- Use only causal change-detection (e.g., BOCPD-style) for live inputs; offline methods are research labels only.
- Flag any statement requiring intra-bar order as `undetermined_at_scale` unless finer data is available and causal.
