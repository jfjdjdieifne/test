# 15 — Contextual Selection Method (R2)

Status: research method for a future implementation. No thresholds, weights, or trading decisions are given here. Where a parameter is needed it is named and left as a declared policy slot. Claim IDs: `CSM-*` (this file), `SP-*` (file 17), `SWS-*` (file 16), `LRN-*` (file 18).

## 15.1 Definitions

- **Selection.** A declared-task choice of one or more candidate elements, or an explicit **unresolved state**. A selection is never a bare winner: it carries the task, the representations used, the exclusions applied, and the justification chain.
- **Task card** `T`: question · downstream purpose · scale class · hard requirements `H_T` · exclusion rules `X_T` · comparison basis `C_T` · resolution policy `Π_T` (may be "none").
- **Candidate.** An element generated by a causal generator (file 02, 04, 05) with its representation ID, parameters, `occurrence_bar`, `knowable_bar`, and status history.
- **Representation** `R`: family + scale + parameters + causal mode. Two candidates are comparable only within the same representation class unless a cross-representation rule is declared.

## 15.3 Property classes

Every property used in a selection carries exactly one class:

| Class | Meaning | Example | May establish relevance alone? |
|---|---|---|---|
| **F** factual | Deterministic function of observed bars and stated definitions | Candidate knowable at bar t; leg B's endpoints inside leg A's price span | Yes, when necessary for the task |
| **O** operational policy | A declared choice with no market-truth claim | Scale class for "structural"; tolerance for clusters; distance units | Only as a declared policy, recorded as such |
| **S** source-declared | Stated by an ICT source (provenance and grade recorded) | "old low on a higher timeframe" as a support reference (P1) | Only as the source's stated condition, not as an observed fact |
| **L** learned | Estimated from data or annotations | Annotator-consistent preference between two plausible references | Only with the learned-model evidence in file 18 |
| **U** unknown | Needed but not available | Whether the source's "near support" means overlap | Forces an unresolved state |

Rule: a property's class is part of its name in the output. A candidate reported with `relevant=true` and no class is invalid.

## 15.4 Relations that establish relevance directly

Relations that can establish task relevance without further policy, when the task's definition makes them necessary:

1. **Knowability** (F): a candidate used at decision bar `t` must have `knowable_bar ≤ t`. Necessary for every task. Not sufficient for relevance.
2. **Binding by crossing** (F): a closed bar `b` crosses reference `r` on the break side. Establishes that `r` was the reference the event touched. Does not establish that the event matters.
3. **Containment** (F): leg `f` is contained in leg `c` when its time interval lies inside `c`'s and both endpoints lie within `c`'s price span (SP-05). Establishes nesting. Does not establish that the fine leg is irrelevant.
4. **Falsifier definition** (F given I): if interpretation I asserts "reference r in view V holds", then a close beyond `r` in the opposite direction falsifies that assertion. Establishes the falsifier for I, given I is written in these terms.
5. **Taken status** (F): a later bar traded through a level (wick) or closed beyond it (close). Establishes state, not cause.
6. **Source-declared validation** (S): trade-through of an order-block candle's high validates it under the primary text read. Establishes the source's validation state only.

Relations that **cannot** establish relevance alone: age, touch count, candle size, distance, "clean" look, number of confluences. These may be features for learning or policy, but a selection that rests only on them is an unresolved or policy-declared selection, and must say so.

## 15.5 Exclusion rules

Exclusion is task-specific and logged. Canonical exclusion reasons:

- `X-KNOW`: not knowable at decision bar.
- `X-SCALE`: scale class incompatible with the task card.
- `X-SUPER`: superseded by a more extreme same-side point inside the confirmation window (for provisional points, SWS-04).
- `X-STATE`: status incompatible (e.g., already invalidated for this task).
- `X-GEOM`: geometric impossibility given the task (e.g., an invalidation level on the wrong side of the current price for the declared interpretation).
- `X-DOM`: dominated by another candidate under §15.6.
- `X-POL`: excluded by an explicit resolution policy `Π_T` (must name the policy ID).

Excluded candidates remain in the output's excluded set. A downstream consumer can inspect them. Never delete.

## 15.6 Comparison without arbitrary importance

**Dominance filter.** For task `T` with a declared list of required properties `q_1 … q_m` (each F or O, with a stated direction where needed), let `S(A)` be the set of properties candidate `A` satisfies. `A` dominates `B` iff `S(B) ⊂ S(A)` strictly. The dominated candidate is excluded under `X-DOM`. Properties are boolean, not scored. Ties (`S(A) = S(B)`) remain. This removes candidates that are strictly worse on the declared requirements without assigning weights.

What dominance cannot do: when `A` satisfies `q_1` and `B` satisfies `q_2`, neither dominates. This is the common case for real alternatives and must be reported as unresolved unless a declared resolution policy exists.

**Within-class comparison.** Inside the surviving set, comparisons use only factual differences (e.g., which endpoint is more recent by `knowable_bar`). Declared orderings (e.g., "most recent first") are policies, not facts, and must be named in `Π_T`.

## 15.7 Justified selections and unresolved states

**Justified selection** requires all of:
1. Hard requirements `H_T` satisfied by the selected candidate (F or S, recorded).
2. Each excluded candidate has an `X-*` reason.
3. Either exactly one survivor, or exactly one survivor after dominance, or a survivor chosen by a declared `Π_T` (named, recorded, sensitivity-tested).
4. Provenance of every step recorded.

**Unresolved state** is emitted when survivors remain and no `Π_T` applies. Each unresolved state carries one reason code:

| Code | Meaning | What would resolve it |
|---|---|---|
| **MO** | Missing market observation (data not present, e.g., intrabar order, volume, a higher-timeframe bar not closed) | Obtain the observation (finer data, closed bar, volume) |
| **MP** | Missing operational policy (the task card leaves a choice open) | Declare a policy `Π_T` and run sensitivity analysis |
| **AI** | Ambiguous ICT interpretation (source wording admits several readings) | Source re-reading with timestamps, or an explicit researcher-declared reading recorded as O/S-ambiguous. Not resolvable by data alone. |
| **IC** | Insufficient contextual evidence (too few candidates, too short history, reference not yet confirmed) | More history or confirmation |
| **EP** | Empirical preference not yet learned (the choice is well-defined but no evidence says which option is better for the outcome) | Causal evaluation against matched controls (file 18) |
| **OOD** | Out-of-distribution state (the current market state lies outside the regime, instrument, or scale covered by validation) | Coverage extension; or abstention is the correct output |

Rule: do not call every unresolved state a defect. MO and IC often indicate missing data; AI and EP indicate that the knowledge is genuinely open; OOD indicates that any learned output is unreliable there.

## 15.8 Dependent conclusions

| Selection | Conclusions that change when it changes |
|---|---|
| Structural reference (T-S1, file 16) | Break interpretation; invalidation; zone context; which liquidity candidates are "objective" |
| Event binding (T-S2) | Which reference the event is attributed to; which view's state changes |
| Entry-timing leg (T-S3) | Zone boundaries derived from it; fine-scale liquidity below/above it |
| Retracement base (T-S4) | Depth comparisons; any zone built from the retracement |
| Invalidation reference (T-S5) | The falsifier for I; the stop-side reference for zones built on I |
| Scale class (T-S6) | Which of the above are evaluated at all |
| Liquidity candidate relevance (LQ tasks) | Which levels are "targets"; taken status interpretation |
| Zone candidate relevance (ZN tasks) | Which zones are shown as relevant; their status |

Dependency rule: a conclusion `C` depends on selection `s` if `C`'s task card lists `s` as an input. Changing `s` invalidates `C` and every conclusion that lists `C` as an input. Recompute in topological order; never patch in place (file 11, IC-11).

## 15.9 Evaluating coherent combinations without unbounded search

Joint selections are needed (a structural reference and an entry leg are chosen together), but enumerating all combinations is not justified.

Procedure:

1. **Order tasks topologically** by §15.8 (scale and structure first, then events, then liquidity and zones, then invalidation).
2. **Select sequentially with propagation.** Resolve each task with §15.6–15.7. If a task yields an unresolved set `U_τ`, carry all members forward. Do not pick one to continue.
3. **Prune by constraints.** For each partial combination, check the factual logical constraints (SP-07). Inconsistent partial combinations are excluded with a recorded reason.
4. **Bound the branching.** The surviving combinations are capped by an explicit budget `B`. The value of `B` is an operational policy with no default in this foundation. If the budget is exceeded, the output reports the unresolved components and the count, rather than a sample of combinations.
5. **Report three separate status flags** for each surviving combination:
   - **Logical consistency**: pass / fail / not evaluable, from factual constraints (SP-07).
   - **ICT-source fidelity**: pass / fail / not applicable, from source-declared constraints (S-class properties) with their provenance. Pass means "consistent with the stated procedure", not "correct".
   - **Empirical predictive usefulness**: not evaluated in the selection step. It is only ever a separate learned or measured property (file 18, objective C).

These three are not equivalent. A combination can be logically consistent and ICT-faithful yet have no demonstrated predictive value, and vice versa for a learned selector.

## 15.10 Avoiding circular definitions

Layers, with one-way dependencies:

- **L0** bars (observed).
- **L1** turning-point candidates (from L0 only).
- **L2** legs (pairs of L1 points).
- **L3** nesting relations (containment, escape) among L2 legs at different scales.
- **L4** structure labels (sequences of L2 legs under a declared view).
- **L5** events (crossings, reclaims, completions) against L4 references.
- **L6** task selections, liquidity and zone candidates, interpretations.

No layer may read a higher layer. In particular, "structure" may not be defined using order blocks or liquidity; "impulse" or "correction" labels are L6 interpretations and may not define L1–L4. The circularity check: for each generator, list its inputs and confirm all lie in lower layers. Violations are logged as defects.

## 15.11 Where learning enters

Learning may only supply `L`-class properties and abstention behavior (file 18). It may not redefine a deterministic generator, and its outputs must pass the same hard requirements and logical checks as any other candidate.

## 15.12 Summary of the three non-equivalent properties

| Property | Question | Evidence used | Where it lives |
|---|---|---|---|
| Logical consistency | Do the selected elements satisfy the task's declared requirements and each other's geometric constraints? | F and O properties | SP-07, §15.9 flag 1 |
| ICT-source fidelity | Is the selection consistent with the ICT source's stated procedure? | S-class properties with provenance | §15.9 flag 2, SP-09/10 |
